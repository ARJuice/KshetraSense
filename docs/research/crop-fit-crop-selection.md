# Crop selection for the Soil Zoning reference report

**Evidence checked:** 2026-10-14  
**Purpose:** Choose three region-relevant crop profiles for the Bengaluru-adjacent synthetic zoning demos. This is not a crop recommendation or a suitability assessment.

## Recommendation

Use **Finger millet (ragi), Tomato, and Groundnut** in Soil Zoning’s factor-by-factor Crop Fit report. Keep this list independent from Leaf Diagnosis: its Apple/Tomato/Potato classes describe the image model’s labels, not the crops that a soil report must compare.

These three have a stronger Eastern Dry Zone/Karnataka context than the current Apple/Tomato/Potato trio. ICAR lists finger millet and groundnut among Karnataka’s important crops; UAS Bengaluru’s Agricultural Research Station at Chintamani focuses on groundnut and tests ragi and other dryland crops. A UAS Bengaluru study also documents intensive tomato cultivation in Chintamani/Chikkaballapur. [ICAR: Karnataka](https://icar.gov.in/en/node/17269), [UAS Bengaluru: ARS Chintamani](https://www.uasbangalore.edu.in/en/ars-chintamani/), [UAS Bengaluru tomato-soil study](https://www.uasbangalore.edu.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/4.pdf)

The ten paired demo inputs are explicitly synthetic, method-unspecified, illustrative points near Bengaluru, with five samples each at a common 0–15 cm depth. Their values are not farm observations or laboratory measurements. [Demo data README](../../data/demo/soil-zoning/README.md)

The original zone smoke-test (FCM, seed 42, maxK 6, 10 m cells, merge threshold 0.5, no perturbation runs) showed three near-neutral zone means in the narrow overlap of all three cited pH intervals (6.5–6.6): 6.54 in `balanced-reference`, 6.52 in `high-nutrient-mixed-ec`, and 6.57 in `mixed-nutrient-field`. To improve demo contrast, every pH value in those three bundles was translated uniformly by -0.10 pH. No other sample field changed. A uniform translation leaves each field's z-score-normalized pH surface and resulting cluster/zone assignments unchanged; pH-derived SHI values move slightly, so the data remain labeled synthetic and the SHI remains provisional. Re-running the current FCM/IDW pipeline confirms no zone mean lies inside all three crop pH contexts simultaneously. This is a deliberate demonstration choice, not an agronomic correction; individual cell estimates can still fall in the interval overlap. Do not modify EC/N/P/K to create crop-specific states because the sample method, analyte, and local calibration are not established.

The current pH scenarios retain contrasts: `acidic-low-nutrients` is 4.8–5.5, `balanced-reference` is 5.9–6.5, `alkaline-high-phosphorus` is 7.6–8.1, and `contrasting-ph-patches` spans 5.0–7.8. These cover values below and above the Tomato and Groundnut pH contexts, while much of the Ragi source context is intentionally broad. Do not narrow Ragi's cited context to manufacture an out-of-range state.

## Crop-specific context the report can safely show

| Crop | Local relevance | Published pH context | Salinity context |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Finger millet (ragi)** | A Karnataka-important crop; UAS Bengaluru lists ragi among dryland crops tested at Chintamani. An ICAR-hosted study of four finger-millet-growing Karnataka pedons observed pH from 4.85 at Sidlaghatta to 6.88 at Hunsur; these are observed sites, **not** a target interval. | NIFTEM-T’s millet technical report says finger millet grows best at pH 4.5–8.0. Treat this broad, method-unspecified statement as source context only; do not turn it into a pass/fail rule. [NIFTEM-T report](https://niftem-t.ac.in/pmfme/maltdpr.pdf), [ICAR-hosted Karnataka soil study](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/JISSS/article/view/157563) | FAO’s table has no finger-millet-specific threshold. Do not substitute a value for channel millet or foxtail millet; they are different crops. [FAO Annex 1](https://www.fao.org/4/y4263e/y4263e0e.htm) |
| **Tomato** | UAS Bengaluru research describes tomato as prominent in Chintamani and cultivated in the Eastern Dry Zone. | TNAU’s tomato guide gives pH 6.5–7.5 for well-drained loamy soil rich in organic matter. This is a production-guide context from Tamil Nadu, not a Karnataka-calibrated threshold; the local UAS study describes sampled tomato soils as slightly acidic, illustrating why source context matters. [TNAU tomato guide](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/horti_vegetables_tomato_Soil.html), [UAS Bengaluru tomato-soil study](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/JISSS/article/view/166022) | FAO reports a tomato fruit-yield ECe threshold of 2.5 dS/m and a 9.9%/dS/m slope above it. This is a salinity yield-response reference, not a generic EC target. [FAO Annex 1](https://www.fao.org/4/y4263e/y4263e0e.htm) |
| **Groundnut** | UAS Bengaluru’s Chintamani station conducts location-specific groundnut research for Karnataka’s Eastern Dry Zone. ICAR also documents groundnut varieties recommended for Karnataka. [UAS Bengaluru: ARS Chintamani](https://www.uasbangalore.edu.in/en/ars-chintamani/), [ICAR: Groundnut varieties](https://www.icar.org.in/en/crop-science/groundnut) | TNAU’s crop-acidity table lists 5.3–6.6 as an optimum pH range for groundnut. It does not specify a test method or local cultivar calibration; use it only as a cited reference, not a universal boundary. [TNAU: Problem soils and their management](https://www.agritech.tnau.ac.in/pdf/3.pdf) | FAO reports a peanut/groundnut seed-yield ECe threshold of 3.2 dS/m and slope of 29%/dS/m above it. This is conditional salinity-response context, not a planting verdict. [FAO Annex 1](https://www.fao.org/4/y4263e/y4263e0e.htm) |

FAO defines these salt-response values using the electrical conductivity of a saturated-paste extract averaged over the crop root zone (ECe). Its compilation combines experiments under differing conditions and says values are guidelines. The demo CSV’s generic EC has no verified extractant or root-zone sampling, so **do not compare its EC directly to these thresholds or assign an EC status**.

## Limits that matter for the current demo contract

- Keep labels such as **“published pH context”** and **“ECe yield-response reference”**; avoid calling every interval “normal,” “good,” or “suitable.” These values come from different sources, crop systems, and evidence types.
- The demos report N/P/K as generic mg/kg with `lab_method` explicitly unspecified. The local tomato-soil study reports available N, P₂O₅, and K₂O in kg/ha; fertilizer application rates are also not soil-test sufficiency ranges. Do not invent or convert crop-specific N/P/K bands without analyte, extractant, reporting basis, sample layer, and local calibration.
- The shared 0–15 cm sample layer does not characterize the full root zone, texture, drainage, irrigation, season, or cultivar. Groundnut in particular needs crop- and depth-aware context; the ICAR-hosted Deccan farm-scale study assessed multiple soil series and depths of 25–150 cm rather than treating surface chemistry alone as a verdict. [ICAR-hosted Deccan groundnut study](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/JISSS/article/view/145955)
- Zone means and cell values remain the existing area-weighted IDW summaries/estimates. The report should show them transparently and leave the planting decision to the user; no crop ranking, probability, or overall fit label follows from these references.

## Potato alternative

**Tomato + Potato + Ragi is defensible** if keeping two Leaf Diagnosis host crops in the soil comparison has a clear demonstration purpose. UAS Bengaluru’s KVK Hassan lists potato among its demonstration crops, and a UAS Bengaluru field study in Hassan reports a recommended soil pH of 5.5 (with growth impairment below 4.8); FAO gives a potato tuber-yield ECe threshold of 1.7 dS/m and a 12%/dS/m slope. [UAS Bengaluru: KVK Hassan](https://www.uasbangalore.edu.in/en/krishi-vigyan-kendra-hassan/), [UAS Bengaluru potato study](https://www.uasbangalore.edu.in/images/2021-1st-Issue/9.pdf), [FAO Annex 1](https://www.fao.org/4/y4263e/y4263e0e.htm)

For a locally representative Eastern Dry Zone trio, Groundnut is the stronger third profile than Potato. The potato sources establish that it is a legitimate Karnataka crop, not that it is as representative as ragi/groundnut for these synthetic Bengaluru-adjacent field scenarios.
