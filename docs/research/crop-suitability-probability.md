# Crop suitability percentages: evidence note

**Conclusion:** Do not show potato, tomato, and apple as probabilities that sum to 100% for a zone. The current zoning and crop-rule design cannot support that claim. Keep suitability/compatibility separate from probability of successful cultivation.

## What the methods mean

K-means and Fuzzy C-means group soil profiles by similarity; they do not predict crop outcomes. FCM assigns each point a membership in each cluster, so a field's FCM membership is about cluster assignment, not the probability that a crop will grow. [scikit-fuzzy FCM documentation](https://scikit-fuzzy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/auto_examples/plot_cmeans.html).

FAO land evaluation matches the requirements of a specified land-use type (crop, management and input assumptions) against land characteristics and limitations. It assigns suitability orders/classes—such as Suitable/Not Suitable and S1, S2, S3, N1, N2—not probability of success. Factor thresholds, interactions and stated input/improvement assumptions are part of that evaluation; yield and cost evidence may be needed for quantitative evaluation. [FAO framework classification](https://www.fao.org/4/x5648e/x5648e0j.htm), [FAO matching and class assignment](https://www.fao.org/4/X5648E/x5648e08.htm).

FAO also documents semi-quantitative suitability indices, for example products of factor ratings or a most-limiting-factor rating. Those are rule-based ratings, not calibrated frequencies. In FAO agro-ecological zoning, suitability considers climate, growing period, water stress, pests/disease, soils, slope, texture and input levels; it may model attainable yield under stated assumptions. A yield index or expected-yield reduction is still not the probability that cultivation succeeds. [FAO semi-quantitative soil suitability](https://www.fao.org/soils-portal/soil-assessment/a-semi-quantitative-approach/en/), [FAO agro-ecological zoning method](https://www.fao.org/4/W2962E/w2962e-04.htm).

## What a defensible suitability assessment needs

FAO's framework first defines a land-use type, then inventories the climate, slope, soils and other physical properties, and matches those properties to crop requirements. Relevant crop/land qualities include growing-period and thermal regimes; radiation and moisture/water availability; soil temperature, moisture, aeration and fertility; effective rooting depth; texture/stoniness; toxicity; slope/topography; flooding; and management/input assumptions. Which are class-determining depends on the crop and production system. The current KshetraSense feature vector (pH, soil temperature, EC, N/P/K, optional contextual moisture) covers only a subset: it lacks seasonal climate/water balance, radiation, rooting depth, texture, drainage/aeration, terrain and crop/management context. It therefore cannot support a full land-suitability classification or cultivation probability. [FAO land-evaluation procedure](https://www.fao.org/4/T0715E/t0715e06.htm), [FAO crop edaphic inventory and AEZ steps](https://www.fao.org/4/W2962E/w2962e-04.htm).

| Assessment dimension | Details to define or measure | Current KshetraSense coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crop/use definition | Crop and cultivar/rootstock where relevant; production purpose; growing season; rainfed vs irrigated; input and management level; assumed improvements. | Not defined yet. |
| Climate and water | Seasonal air-temperature/thermal regime, frost and other hazards, radiation/day length, growing-period length, rainfall and water balance/availability. | Soil temperature and an optional point moisture snapshot only; not enough for seasonal availability. |
| Soil/rooting | Soil pH, salinity/EC, nutrient availability with lab method/unit/depth, texture/structure, effective depth, rooting conditions, drainage/aeration, water storage and toxicity where relevant. | Partial: pH, EC, N/P/K and soil temperature; texture, depth, drainage, aeration and several lab-method details are absent. |
| Terrain and land constraints | Slope/topography, flooding/water table, erosion, stones, access/trafficability, and any planned drainage/irrigation/land improvement. | Not represented in the current grid. |
| Measured success (probability only) | Field × crop × season outcomes with a defined success threshold, yield/harvest and matched conditions/management; representative failures as well as successes. | None: the grid is synthetic and zoning has no crop-outcome labels. |

This is a screening checklist, not a claim that every field is required for every crop. The assessment must select the relevant class-determining factors for the named land-use type and show unsupported factors as missing, rather than silently treating them as favorable. The FAO workflow matches defined land-use requirements to mapped land characteristics and notes that measured growth/performance data are needed to predict class performance quantitatively. [FAO land-evaluation procedure](https://www.fao.org/4/T0715E/t0715e06.htm), [FAO AEZ factor inventory](https://www.fao.org/4/W2962E/w2962e-04.htm).

Published crop guidance is context-specific rather than one universal cutoff. Examples: Minnesota Extension describes apple soil pH 6–7 and avoidance of standing water; an ICAR Indian advisory specifies slightly acidic loam around pH 6.5–6.7 and depth over 45 cm. Penn State's commercial tomato guidance targets pH 5.8–6.6, while an ICAR polyhouse cherry-tomato article gives pH 6–7. Penn State potato production guidance gives pH 5.5–6.5, while Minnesota Extension describes 6–6.5 as ideal and says potatoes can tolerate down to 5. These are examples, not thresholds for KshetraSense; they illustrate why each profile must state geography, crop/variety, production setting, soil-test method and management assumptions. [UMN apples](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-apples), [ICAR 2025 advisory](https://icar.gov.in/sites/default/files/Circulars/ICAR%20En-Kharif%20Agro-Advisories%20for%20Farmers%202025.pdf), [Penn State tomato production](https://extension.psu.edu/tomato-production), [ICAR tomato article](https://www.icar.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-05/Indian%20Horticulture%2C%20May-June%202026%20Low%20new.pdf), [Penn State potato production](https://extension.psu.edu/potato-production), [UMN potatoes](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-potatoes).

FAO also uses percentages for a different metric: crop/land suitability can be expressed as estimated attainable yield relative to that crop's own maximum or constraint-free yield under a defined location, production system and input level. Its crop suitability classes are based on those per-crop yield ratios. That denominator is not shared among apple, tomato and potato, so the percentages are not shares of one 100% total and are not success probabilities. [FAO crop and land suitability assessment](https://www.fao.org/4/i2713e/i2713e.pdf), [FAO rainfed suitability methodology](https://www.fao.org/4/y4252e/y4252e04.pdf).

## Why three crop probabilities need not total 100%

“This crop succeeds under these conditions” is a separate yes/no event for each crop. More than one crop can be suitable, or none; these events are not mutually exclusive alternatives. Therefore three independently estimated success probabilities need not sum to 100%. Probabilities summing to one apply to an exhaustive set of mutually exclusive outcomes (for example, “which single crop is selected?”), which is a different product question. Scikit-learn's documentation distinguishes single-label probabilities, which sum to one, from multilabel marginal probabilities, which do not. [NIST probability reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/apr/section1/apr1a.htm), [scikit-learn multilabel probability semantics](https://scikit-learn.org/1.9/api/sklearn.multiclass.html).

Normalizing three compatibility scores to force a 100% total would only make a composition of those scores; it would not calibrate them or establish crop-success odds. Calling that output “probability” would be misleading.

## Recommended product representation

- After zoning, show three equally weighted crop cards with factor-by-factor status, observed value and unit, applicable crop-specific range, source/profile version, and missing-data state. Use **“Not assessed”** where the profile or measurement is unsupported. This matches the project’s existing evidence boundary.
- If sourced thresholds and an explicit, tested scoring rule are later approved, an aggregate may be labeled **“soil-profile compatibility index”** or an FAO-style suitability class—not probability, chance of cultivation, yield forecast, or recommendation. State the factors and management assumptions covered.
- If a percentage visual is important, use **within-crop area by suitability class** (e.g. percentage of assessed cells in the selected zone classified S1/S2/S3/N for tomato), after defining the classes. State that denominator and report the not-assessed/missing share separately. Those class shares may sum to 100% only over the declared assessed area for one crop at a time; they describe mapped area, not the relative chance that one of three crops will succeed. Do not make the three crop bars sum to 100%.

## Minimum evidence before using “probability of successful cultivation”

1. Define the event and horizon before modelling—for example, “marketable yield at or above X kg/ha for crop C in one named season under specified management.” “Can be cultivated” alone has no measurable success threshold.
2. Obtain representative, observed field-by-crop-by-season outcomes for the target geography, with matched soil-test method/depth, weather/growing period, crop/variety, planting date, irrigation and other management. Include relevant limitations absent from the current soil vector (such as texture, rooting depth, drainage, and climate) or explicitly narrow the claim to the supported soil factors. FAO’s methodology treats suitability as crop- and management-specific and includes interactions/conditions beyond a few soil nutrients.
3. Fit an outcome model and reserve independent validation data (preferably separate fields/seasons/sites). Calibrate probabilities on data not used to fit the model, inspect reliability diagrams (predicted probability versus observed positive fraction), and report calibration and error measures, per-crop sample counts, uncertainty, validation geography/time, and known extrapolation limits. A model score or classifier `predict_proba` output alone is not proof of calibration. [scikit-learn probability-calibration guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html).
4. Treat the three crop outcomes as separate binary outcomes unless the target is explicitly a single mutually exclusive decision such as “which one crop is chosen.” If reporting independent crop-success probabilities, let them sum to something other than 100% when appropriate. A three-way 100% distribution would need a separate, explicitly defined choice/outcome problem with mutually exclusive classes (and a “none/unknown” outcome where applicable); it is not a reformatted crop-success probability.

**Project implication:** The planned fixed-seed synthetic field has no real crop-success outcomes, and current K-means/FCM zones have no crop label or yield target. Thus no mock or MVP value should be described as a crop cultivation probability. Use transparent, source-backed compatibility/suitability outputs now; revisit calibrated probabilities only after the outcome-data and validation requirements above are met.
